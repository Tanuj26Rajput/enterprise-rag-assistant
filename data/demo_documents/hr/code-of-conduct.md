# Code of Conduct

Owner: People Operations, Helix Labs
Effective: 1 January 2026
Applies to: employees, contractors, interns, and anyone representing Helix
Labs at an external event

## 1. The standard

Treat colleagues, customers and partners with respect. That is the whole
standard; the rest of this document explains what it means in practice and
what happens when it is not met.

## 2. Expected behaviour

- Assume good faith. Most disagreements are about information, not motive.
- Disagree with the argument, not the person. "That design has a race
  condition" is a critique. "You never think things through" is not.
- Give credit accurately and publicly. Take responsibility privately and
  then publicly.
- Respect people's time: come prepared, write things down, and end meetings
  when they are finished.
- Respect people's boundaries: their working hours, their time off, their
  choice of how much of their personal life to share.
- Use people's names and pronouns as they give them, and correct yourself
  without ceremony if you get them wrong.

## 3. Unacceptable behaviour

The following are never acceptable, in any venue connected to work,
including chat, code review, video calls, social events and conferences:

- Harassment, including unwelcome attention of a sexual nature, repeated
  unwanted contact, and deliberate intimidation.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments related to age, disability, ethnicity,
  gender, gender identity or expression, nationality, neurotype, physical
  appearance, race, religion, or sexual orientation.
- Threats or violence of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Sustained disruption of discussions or events.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern under this code.

Retaliation is treated as seriously as the original conduct. A process that
is unsafe to use is not a process.

## 4. Raising a concern

You can raise a concern in any of these ways:

1. Directly with the person, if you feel able to. This is often enough and
   is never required.
2. With your manager, or with any manager if yours is the problem.
3. With People Operations, by message or by email.
4. Anonymously, through the reporting form on the intranet.

You do not need to be the subject of the behaviour to report it, and you do
not need to be certain. Report what you observed.

## 5. What happens next

Every report is acknowledged within two working days.

People Operations appoints an investigator who has no reporting relationship
with anyone involved. The investigator speaks to the person who reported, to
the person reported about, and to any witnesses. Both parties may bring a
colleague to any meeting.

Investigations aim to conclude within 15 working days. Where an
investigation will take longer, both parties are told why and given a
revised date.

The person who raised the concern is told the outcome: whether the conduct
was substantiated and whether action was taken. They are not told the
specific disciplinary measure, which is confidential to the individual.

## 6. Interim measures

Where there is a risk of further harm, People Operations may put interim
measures in place during an investigation, such as changing reporting lines
or pausing shared work. Interim measures are not a finding and are not
disciplinary.

## 7. Consequences

Depending on severity and on whether the behaviour has recurred, outcomes
may include a conversation and an apology, a written warning, a change of
role or team, removal of specific privileges, or termination of employment
or contract.

Serious misconduct, including harassment, threats and retaliation, may
result in immediate termination without a prior warning.

## 8. False reports

A report that is not substantiated is not a false report. A report made in
good faith that turns out to be mistaken carries no consequence whatsoever.

A report made deliberately in bad faith, knowing it to be untrue, is itself
a breach of this code.

## 9. Questions

People Operations owns this code and will answer questions about it in
confidence.
